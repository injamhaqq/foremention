## 2. What Foremention will *not* race incumbents to build during Stage 0

Do not compete first on the size of a scraped prompt index, a generic chat-based marketing agent, rendering different pages to AI crawlers, unauthorized third-party content redistribution, brand-perception vanity scoring, or every possible provider integration. Large competitors can replicate UI and spend on distribution. Those features cannot substitute for Foremention's present proof gaps.

**Narrow proposed category:** a **Buyer Decision Evidence Loop for growth-stage B2B SaaS**. The testable job is: *For five real buying questions, show exactly where a named competitor was recorded and which provider-returned sources were human-reviewed; let the customer approve one legitimate corrective action; repeat the exact comparable observation and record what was and wasn't observed.*

It is not a claim that Foremention already has a defensible moat or that acting on a citation will cause a model to recommend the company.

## 3. Three stages of the product loop

**A. Make the first customer decision concrete (this PR).** The private Buyer-Question Evidence Packet reads the most recent finalized reviewed collection, displays answer-by-answer brand status, literal competitor-name candidates and provider-returned citations, and only labels a cited-page gap after explicit human review for that same run and exact URL. It distinguishes no answers, unreviewed pages, missing models, duplicate question slots and demo data from publishable observations. It adds **zero new providers, tables, migration files, service-role reads, scraping or autonomous outreach**. It is not evidence of improved recommendations.

**B. Make the action a customer decision (existing workflow, next usability experiment).** Inspect the matching Source Map entry, test that the page and rights remain valid, have the customer nominate and approve one controllable change with an owner and timestamp, and preserve the exact evidence snapshot. No speculative citation "cause" and no auto-posting.

**C. Make the repeat inspectable (existing exact-comparability infrastructure and pending PR #352).** Remeasure unchanged question wording/provider/model/methodology and all nine material measurement-context fields. Incomparable means no movement claim. Capture the customer-confirmed decision and operational value separately from observed recommendation direction, with no attribution of revenue without independent evidence.

**Compounding hypothesis—not yet verified:** Each consenting customer who completes both reviewed cycles contributes process knowledge about *which workflow was useful* and what evidence was inspected. Protect tenant boundaries, keep consent and retention rights, never pool proprietary customer answer text as a resale dataset, and test whether that workflow knowledge improves activation or renewals before calling it a moat.

## 4. The initial commercial wedge

- **Target:** English-language B2B SaaS, approximately 50–500 employees, at least three actual comparison competitors, marketing leader with execution capacity.
- **Champion and economic buyer hypotheses:** Head/Director SEO, Organic, GEO/AEO or Growth; budget owner VP Marketing/CMO/VP Growth.
- **Single demonstrable deliverable:** five buyer questions, 3–5 competitors, a reviewed first collection and source map, one customer-owned change, one second *eligible* measurement and an honest noncausal evidence packet.
- **Offer experiments:** the current Stage-0 $3,000 and $6,000 narrow pilot hypotheses require buyer conversations and recorded signed/collected outcomes before any price claim. Do not substitute historical ~$499 monthly hypotheses as confirmed willingness to pay.
- **Demo discipline:** 20 minutes around one real buyer question, one reviewed answer, one cited page and one customer decision; feature tours and fictional demos are not customer proof.
- **Distribution:** founder-led, permission-respecting targeted research and 20–30 qualified conversations. Track real accept/reject reasons and which buyers execute a change. No unsolicited automated message blasts or inflated conversion guarantees.

## 5. Execution sequence and non-negotiable gates

**Immediate:** resolve exposed synthetic production canary credential and session revocation verification (#323), legally acceptable commercial retrieval rights (#346), and provider reliability/preflight (#324/#325). Keep the no-production-push rule until migration lineage (#332, audit PR #354) has complete historical SQL provenance and a verified backup/test restore. Do not merge a broad feature branch to distract from these release blockers.

**Product slice in this PR:** review new Buyer-Question Evidence Packet with one real design partner using *only* licensed, already permitted, finalized verified observations. Confirm the text candidates and reviewed-source joins before any public demo claim. Measure time from opening a customer question to accepting or rejecting one reviewed action—do not invent an improvement percentage.

**Second-cycle slice:** apply PR #352's context-aware follow-up validator only through a separately approved forward migration after the migration-history safety gate. PR #353 supplies isolated authenticated acceptance evidence but does not establish production or provider reliability.

**Evidence threshold:** do not declare Stage 0 complete until 3–5 qualified external partners actively complete the workflow, at least one verified paid pilot or signed commercial commitment is recorded, and at least two organizations finish a second comparable reviewed cycle. At least one customer-confirmed decision outcome should be inspectable with limitations.

**Decision checkpoints:** after 20 qualified interviews, assess whether decision-proof is a sufficiently urgent owned problem; after 30 if consistently low urgency/funding, narrow or change the thesis. If users understand the evidence but never approve an action, simplify the deliverable instead of expanding analytics. Revisit competitors' official releases weekly, but choose product work only when it improves this customer-defined loop.
