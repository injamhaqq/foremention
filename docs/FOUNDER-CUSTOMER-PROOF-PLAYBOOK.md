# Foremention Founder Customer-Proof Playbook

Purpose: turn private-beta product capability into real customer evidence. This is a targeted founder-led sales and discovery system, not a mass-outreach system. Every claim, account, conversation, pilot, payment, and outcome must be real and traceable.

## 1. Initial beachhead ICP

Start narrow: **English-language B2B SaaS companies with 50–500 employees** that:

- sell in a category with at least three direct named competitors;
- depend on high-intent comparison / “best X for Y” buyer questions;
- have enough organic/content maturity to act on source evidence;
- have an in-house SEO, content, growth, organic, or demand-generation owner;
- can ship a content/source intervention inside a 30-day pilot;
- care about ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or similar answer-engine discovery as a measurable acquisition/reputation surface.

Primary daily user/champion: Head/Director of SEO, Content, Organic Growth, GEO/AEO, or Growth Marketing.

Economic buyer: VP Marketing / CMO, or a senior growth leader with budget responsibility.

Triggering pain: “Competitors are being recommended or cited for buyer questions that matter to us, and we cannot explain which sources shape the answer or prove which corrective action worked.”

Current substitute: manual LLM sampling + spreadsheets + Ahrefs/Semrush + an AI-visibility tracker or agency.

Switching reason to test: Foremention preserves the evidence chain from answer → source gap → human decision → intervention → controlled remeasurement rather than stopping at a visibility chart.

Budget source hypothesis: SEO/content/organic growth, marketing intelligence, demand generation, or experimentation budget. This remains a hypothesis until customers confirm it.

## 2. ICP qualification rubric

Score 0–100 before outreach. Do not contact an account below 65 unless there is a strong trigger event.

| Dimension | Points | Qualification evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| High-intent AI buyer questions matter commercially | 20 | Product category has comparison / evaluation questions with real purchase intent. |
| Competitive density | 15 | Three or more credible direct competitors. |
| Team can implement an intervention | 15 | In-house SEO/content/growth owner or execution partner. |
| Current AI-discovery urgency | 15 | Public initiative, executive discussion, AI-search work, referral movement, or observed competitor-answer gap. |
| Evidence-sensitive buyer | 10 | Team cares how conclusions are sourced, not only a score. |
| Measurement fit | 10 | Same controlled questions can be re-run over the pilot. |
| Budget/authority path | 10 | Identifiable economic buyer and plausible marketing budget. |
| 30-day actionability | 5 | At least one meaningful change can ship within the pilot window. |

Disqualify when the company has no clear competitive buyer questions, cannot make changes during the pilot, wants ranking promises, or has no stakeholder willing to own the test.

## 3. Trigger-event criteria

Prioritize accounts when one or more are publicly verifiable:

- newly launched AI-search/GEO/AEO initiative;
- SEO/content leadership hire;
- category or product launch creating new comparison questions;
- major competitor launch or repositioning;
- evidence of material AI referral / answer-engine traffic focus;
- executive/public discussion about ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or AI search;
- obvious repeatable buyer-question gap found during a small public research check.

Never invent a trigger. Record source/date in the research record.

## 4. Target-account research template

Before sending anything, record:

- company + domain;
- employee band;
- category;
- top 3–5 competitors;
- five likely high-intent buyer questions;
- public trigger evidence + date;
- one observed AI recommendation/citation hypothesis, clearly labeled if unverified;
- likely champion role;
- likely economic buyer role;
- current substitute/tooling evidence if public;
- ICP score + qualification reason;
- personalized reason to contact now;
- next action and due date.

Do not copy prospect prompts, customer answers, private URLs, or personal data into PostHog.

## 5. Prospect pain hypotheses to test

1. “We know our AI visibility score, but not why competitors win specific high-intent questions.”
2. “We have citation/source data, but cannot translate it into a defensible action.”
3. “We ship content fixes, then lack a controlled before/after measurement.”
4. “Different model runs disagree, so the team does not trust single-run screenshots.”
5. “We cannot show leadership whether GEO/AEO work materially changed buyer-facing answers.”

A hypothesis becomes product evidence only after discovery behavior, workflow use, or payment supports it.

## 6. Outreach structure

Every message must contain one real reason for relevance. No scraped-blast personalization.

### Cold email — touch 1

**Subject:** AI recommendations for {{category}}

Hi {{first_name}} — I’m researching how B2B SaaS teams measure buyer-facing AI recommendations. {{real trigger or observed public reason}}.

Foremention is built around a narrow workflow: preserve the exact answer/source evidence for a few critical buyer questions, identify the actionable source gap, then re-run the same controlled questions after the team ships a fix.

I’m looking for a small number of design partners for a 30-day proof pilot. If AI search/recommendations are already on your roadmap, would a 20-minute evidence walkthrough be useful?

### Follow-up — touch 2

Quick follow-up with the reason I thought this might matter: {{specific public buyer question / competitor context}}. The useful question for us is not “can we produce another visibility score?” It is “can we make the evidence actionable and prove what changed after the action?”

Worth comparing notes for 20 minutes?

### Follow-up — touch 3

Closing the loop. If this is not a priority, I’ll leave it there. If it is, I can show the exact baseline → source review → intervention → remeasurement workflow and we can decide quickly whether a pilot would produce decision-grade evidence for {{company}}.

No automatic endless sequence. Stop after three unanswered touches unless a real new trigger occurs.

## 7. LinkedIn sequence

1. Connection note: one sentence tied to a real trigger; no pitch wall.
2. After acceptance: ask one discovery question about how the team currently measures AI recommendations/citations.
3. If pain is acknowledged: offer the 20-minute evidence walkthrough.
4. If no response after one useful follow-up: stop.

## 8. Discovery call — 30 minutes

### First 5 minutes — context

- What buyer questions matter most before someone chooses your product?
- Which AI answer engines matter to your team today?
- Who owns this problem internally?

### Next 10 minutes — current job and pain

- How do you currently check whether you or competitors appear?
- What do you do when a competitor wins a question?
- How often do you repeat the check?
- What part of the result do you trust least?
- What evidence does leadership need before approving work?
- What happens commercially if this stays unresolved?

### Next 10 minutes — process, urgency, spend

- Which tools/agencies are used now and what do they cost?
- What changed recently that makes this worth solving now?
- Can the team ship a source/content intervention in the next 30 days?
- Who must approve a paid pilot?
- What result would make the pilot worth paying for and continuing?

### Final 5 minutes — qualify next step

Either schedule the evidence demo with the right stakeholders, define a pilot candidate, or mark the account disqualified/later. Never force an opportunity forward without pain + owner + actionability.

## 9. Discovery repository fields

Capture separately:

**What customers say:** current job-to-be-done, current process, alternative, pain, frequency, severity, economic impact, spend, stakeholder, urgency, purchase trigger, trust/evidence/reporting requirements, desired outcome, WTP signal, objections, feature requests, must-have, nice-to-have.

**What customers do:** workflows completed, source reviews performed, decisions created, interventions applied, remeasurements completed, team invitations, repeated use, response time to next action.

**What customers pay for:** quoted price, negotiated price, accepted price, verified paid value, renewal/expansion, and which deliverables were explicitly valuable.

## 10. Demo structure — 20 minutes

1. Start with one real buyer question and the business consequence of the answer.
2. Show the preserved answer/citation/source evidence boundary.
3. Show Source X-Ray and the human review that turns evidence into a decision.
4. Show the governed intervention record.
5. Show how exact comparable remeasurement is accepted or withheld.
6. Show outcome limitations; explicitly separate movement from causation.
7. Close by mapping their five pilot questions, owner, intervention window, and success criteria.

Do not spend the demo touring every feature.

## 11. 30-day proof pilot

Goal: produce one honest, decision-grade before/action/after evidence chain and test willingness to pay.

### Standard hypothesis — USD 3,000

- 5 critical buyer questions;
- up to 3 provider families where technically supported and comparable;
- 3 direct competitors;
- baseline evidence collection;
- Source X-Ray review and prioritized intervention candidate;
- one customer-owned intervention track;
- two controlled remeasurement checkpoints where provider/model/methodology comparability permits;
- final evidence readout with limitations.

### Expanded hypothesis — USD 6,000

- up to 10 critical buyer questions;
- up to 4 provider families where supported;
- up to 5 competitors;
- two intervention tracks;
- two controlled remeasurement checkpoints;
- executive readout + working session.

These are **pricing hypotheses**, not validated willingness to pay and not public pricing. Quote one hypothesis deliberately; record objection, negotiation, accepted value, and paid value. Do not publish an annual ACV until repeated paid evidence exists.

### Pilot success criteria

Agree before starting:

- baseline questions/providers are fixed and recorded;
- at least one reviewed actionable source gap is found, or the pilot truthfully concludes no actionable gap was proven;
- customer ships at least one documented intervention if evidence supports it;
- comparable remeasurement is attempted under the exact measurement contract;
- observed change is reported with limitations, never upgraded to causality without evidence;
- commercial close asks whether the customer will pay to continue monitoring/iteration.

## 12. Pilot proposal structure

1. Customer context and verified pain.
2. Five critical buyer questions.
3. Providers/models and comparability boundary.
4. Baseline scope.
5. Source review / intervention workflow.
6. Remeasurement dates.
7. Success criteria and evidence language.
8. Customer responsibilities and Foremention responsibilities.
9. Pilot price + payment terms.
10. Security/privacy boundary.
11. Renewal/continuation decision date.
12. Explicit exclusions and limitations.

## 13. Objection handling

**“We already use Semrush/Ahrefs/a GEO tracker.”**
Good — the pilot is not asking you to discard monitoring. The test is whether Foremention can preserve a stronger source/action/remeasurement evidence chain for a handful of commercially important questions.

**“AI answers change every run.”**
That is exactly why single screenshots are weak evidence. Foremention should withhold comparisons when the provider/model/methodology boundary is not defensible and use repeated comparable measurements where possible.

**“Can you promise our recommendation share will improve?”**
No. The pilot tests whether we can diagnose a credible gap, support an intervention, and measure the subsequent answer honestly. Improvement cannot be promised.

**“Why pay for a pilot while beta is free?”**
Free beta is product access. The paid pilot hypothesis covers a tightly scoped, founder-assisted proof process, baseline design, evidence review, intervention measurement, and final readout. If that is not valuable enough to fund, that is important pricing evidence.

## 14. Commercial pipeline contract

Account lifecycle:

`target → qualified → contacted → conversation → design_partner/customer` with `disqualified` or `churned` when appropriate.

Sales opportunity stages:

`identified → discovery → demo → pilot_proposed → pilot_active → won/lost`.

Commercial event evidence records outreach, reply, conversation/discovery, demo, pilot proposal/start/completion, payment, renewal, expansion, churn, and customer-success checkpoints.

### Next-action rules

- Every qualified account has one owner and next action.
- Every live opportunity has a dated next action; no passive “follow up someday.”
- After a conversation, update pain, authority, urgency, success criteria, and next action the same day.
- After a demo, either move to a scoped proposal, schedule a defined follow-up, or close/later.
- A pilot does not become `won` without accepted/paid commercial evidence.
- No internal/synthetic organization enters CEO KPIs.

### Lost-deal reason taxonomy

`no_urgent_pain`, `no_budget`, `timing`, `no_authority`, `missing_capability`, `trust_security`, `competitor`, `internal_build`, `no_response`, `other`.

Review lost reasons weekly. Product work is triggered by repeated qualified losses, not one-off requests.

## 15. First case-study measurement framework

### T0 — baseline

Record customer context, five priority buyer questions, provider/model/methodology identity, recommendation/competitor presence, citations/sources, reviewed gaps, and evidence limitations.

### ACTION

Record the intervention, reviewed reason/evidence, approval, date, owner, and where it was applied.

### T1 / T2 / T3 — remeasurement

Re-run the controlled question/provider/model/methodology matrix where technically comparable. Record gains/losses, competitor changes, citation/source changes, stability, and limitations. If exact comparability fails, mark the observation incomparable instead of manufacturing a delta.

### Reporting language

Use only the strongest justified label: Observed change → Correlated change → Plausibly influenced change → Causally established change. A normal pilot should rarely claim causality.

A public case study requires explicit customer permission and real customer evidence. Never substitute a synthetic example for traction.

## 16. 30-day customer-proof sprint

These are targets, not achievements:

**100 target accounts → 40 conversations → 15 demos → 5 pilot proposals → 3 design partners → 1 paid pilot.**

### Week 1 — qualify and learn

- Research first 30 accounts; only send to ICP-qualified accounts.
- Start 8–12 highly personalized outreach actions per working day.
- Run discovery calls immediately when available.
- Synthesize the top pain/objection patterns after every five real conversations.
- Do not build a requested feature unless it blocks a qualified pilot or repeats across multiple strong accounts.

### Week 2 — demos and pilot design

- Reach 50–60 cumulative researched accounts.
- Demo only against the prospect’s buyer questions and evidence requirements.
- Produce the first 2–3 serious pilot proposals.
- Quote one pricing hypothesis; do not automatically discount.
- Lock baseline/success criteria for any design partner.

### Week 3 — close and baseline

- Reach 80 cumulative researched accounts.
- Drive decision-ready proposals to yes/no.
- Target 3 active design partners and at least one paid commitment.
- Run the real T0 baseline for every activated pilot.
- Review the first actionable evidence with the customer and agree one intervention.

### Week 4 — intervention, remeasurement, proof

- Complete 100 carefully selected target accounts.
- Customer ships the agreed intervention where evidence supports it.
- Run T1/T2 comparable remeasurement as timing allows.
- Record observed outcomes and limitations.
- Ask directly for continuation/renewal and permission to develop a case study if evidence is strong.
- End the month with a written ICP/pricing/pain/win-loss synthesis based only on real conversations and behavior.

## 17. Daily founder sales scorecard

Record real counts only:

- target accounts researched;
- qualified;
- contacted;
- replies;
- conversations held;
- demos held;
- pilot proposals sent;
- design partners active;
- paid pilots;
- next actions overdue;
- activated pilot organizations;
- weekly retained value organizations;
- completed comparable remeasurements;
- verified outcome improvements;
- pipeline value / weighted pipeline;
- verified MRR/ARR;
- AI cost for KPI-eligible organizations;
- gross margin only when revenue + cost data are complete.

## 18. Product prioritization rule during the sprint

P0: blocks real customer/revenue/security/release.

P1: materially improves customer proof, activation, repeat value, or outcome integrity.

P2: useful after proof.

P3: defer.

Build only when it blocks a live pilot, repeats across qualified prospects, materially improves activation/value/retention, protects evidence integrity, or resolves a material economics/security/reliability issue.

The sprint succeeds by learning what real customers act on and pay for—not by increasing feature count.
