# Foremention Founder Research Operations

Purpose: collect first-party ICP, problem, buyer and category evidence without leading the participant or turning the research system into a parallel CRM.

## Operating boundary

The canonical account/contact/opportunity/activity objects remain the service-only commercial system. Research sessions and evidence link back to those objects. Product analytics is for privacy-safe product behavior and must not receive raw interview text, names, emails, objections, pricing conversations or commercial notes.

**Do not send** unsolicited outreach from this program until the founder explicitly approves the target list, message and sending scope. Drafts, segmentation and recommended recipients may be prepared before approval.

## Target account rubric

Research priority is based on relevance, not volume. Score each area 0–2 for recruitment triage only; this is not ICP validation proof.

1. Considered B2B purchase with meaningful competition.
2. Important discovery/comparison/shortlist buyer questions exist.
3. AI-mediated recommendations plausibly affect how buyers research the category.
4. Marketing/growth/organic/product-marketing team can act on evidence.
5. Recent public or first-party trigger suggests attention now.
6. Participant role has direct knowledge of the job/problem.
7. Current workaround can be described from recent behavior.
8. Account can realistically run a comparable repeated measurement if qualified.

Use the score to prioritize recruitment. Do not convert a high score into “validated ICP.”

## Contact relevance

Prioritize people who are likely to have performed or owned the job:

- CMO;
- VP Marketing;
- VP Growth;
- Head/Director of SEO;
- Head/Director Organic Growth;
- Product Marketing leadership;
- another role only when evidence indicates direct ownership.

Record role as observed. Do not pre-label every senior marketer as economic buyer.

## Screener

Before scheduling a full research call, establish:

1. What company/product do you work on and what is your role?
2. Do buyers in your category use AI assistants or AI search for discovery, comparisons or recommendations, to your knowledge?
3. Have you personally checked, discussed, measured or acted on those recommendations in the last six months?
4. What happened that caused you or your team to look?
5. Were you directly involved in the response or decision?

A “yes” to AI interest without a real incident is not enough for a strongly ICP-relevant interview.

## Interview script

Open with context: this is research about how teams handle AI-mediated buyer recommendations; there is no need to agree with the premise and negative examples are useful.

### Recent behavior

- Tell me about the last time this happened.
- What triggered you to look?
- What did you do?
- Who cared?
- What happened next?
- What tools did you use?
- What did that cost in time, money or coordination?
- How often does this come up?
- What happens if you do nothing?

### Ownership and buying motion

- Who normally owns the problem?
- Who else becomes involved?
- Who can approve a change in process or tooling?
- Who approved spending?
- What would procurement/security/legal need to see?
- What would make you switch from the current approach?

### Decision criteria and alternatives

- What is good enough about the current approach?
- What is frustrating or missing?
- Which tools, agencies, consultants or internal processes do you use?
- When would you choose to do nothing?
- What would have to be true for this to become urgent?

### Category — unaided first

Before explaining Foremention, ask:

“What would you expect a product called Recommendation Intelligence to do?”

Record the response verbatim before explanation.

Then describe the problem without the category name and ask how the participant would describe the product/category themselves.

### Category/message response

After unaided language is preserved, test the scheduled variant. Ask the participant to explain what they think it means before asking for a 1–5 rating on comprehension, relevance, urgency, differentiation, credibility and action intent.

### Willingness to change / trial / pay

Avoid “Would you use Foremention?” Ask for behavior and tradeoffs:

- What would you need to stop or change to adopt a workflow like this?
- What data/access/time would you be willing to commit to test it?
- Who would need to participate?
- What outcome would justify continuing after the first cycle?
- When you last bought a tool for a similar job, how was budget decided?
- What budget source would this compete with?
- At what point would a commercial discussion be appropriate?

Do not turn a hypothetical price reaction into a payment fact.

### Close

Ask:

- Is there anyone else who deals with this more directly?
- May I follow up if we need to clarify what you described?
- If the problem is real for your team, what would be the most useful next step?

A referral, data commitment, workflow commitment, pilot step or explicit commercial next step is stronger evidence than praise.

## Evidence extraction

Create separate atomic evidence rows rather than one flattering summary. For each item record:

- truth classification;
- confidence dimension;
- detailed dimension;
- direction: supports / weakens / contradicts / neutral;
- evidence kind;
- concise statement;
- verbatim language when useful;
- normalized value when useful;
- observed time;
- source link/reference.

Examples of separate evidence items from one interview: current workaround, trigger event, observed champion role, budget owner, objection, unaided category language, willingness-to-trial commitment. One interview can strengthen one hypothesis and contradict another.

## Objection taxonomy

Start with these codes but preserve the participant’s words:

- no urgent pain;
- unclear owner;
- no budget;
- timing;
- existing workaround good enough;
- missing capability;
- evidence/accuracy concern;
- causality/attribution concern;
- trust/security/privacy;
- procurement complexity;
- category confusion;
- unclear differentiation;
- competitor/alternative preferred;
- internal build;
- no response;
- other/discovered.

Do not force new objections into a convenient old code.

## Win/loss taxonomy

### Won/advanced reasons

- urgent trigger;
- clear owned problem;
- evidence depth valued;
- comparable remeasurement valued;
- action/ownership workflow valued;
- replaces costly manual process;
- competitive pressure;
- executive demand;
- trusted champion;
- acceptable commercial/procurement path;
- other/discovered.

### Lost/stalled reasons

- no urgent pain;
- no recognizable owner;
- no budget;
- current workaround sufficient;
- category/message confusion;
- missing capability;
- insufficient trust/security;
- procurement/timing;
- competitor;
- internal build;
- no response;
- other/discovered.

A deal is not “won” until the canonical commercial stage/evidence supports it. Payment is separate and must be verified.

## Design-partner operating path

Use one canonical path:

`target account → contact → conversation → research call → qualified design partner → onboarding → activation → first reviewed Recommendation Record → evidence-backed action → owner → comparable second cycle → outcome review → commercial discussion → verified payment → retained cycle`

Rules:

- application submitted ≠ accepted design partner;
- accepted design partner ≠ activated;
- proposal ≠ payment;
- configured checkout ≠ collected revenue;
- first cycle ≠ retention;
- second comparable cycle is required before repeat-usage confidence increases;
- payment state stays fail-closed unless verified by a trusted source.

## Outreach drafts

These are drafts only. **Do not send** without explicit founder approval of targets/message/scope.

### Research email

Subject: Quick research question on AI buyer recommendations

Hi {{first_name}},

I’m researching how B2B marketing teams handle an increasingly specific problem: important buyers asking AI systems for category recommendations, comparisons and shortlists — and the team needing to understand what was recommended, what evidence appeared behind it, and what to do next.

I’m not looking for a product endorsement. I’d value 20–25 minutes on the last time your team encountered this, including cases where it was not important enough to act on.

Would you be open to a short research conversation?

— Injam

### LinkedIn-style research note

Hi {{first_name}} — I’m interviewing B2B marketing/growth leaders about the last time AI-driven discovery or recommendation results became a real work problem for their team. No pitch required; negative examples are useful too. Would you be open to a 20-minute research conversation?

### Follow-up 1

Hi {{first_name}}, following up once because your role looks close to the workflow I’m studying. The useful question is simply: when AI recommendations or comparisons matter for buyers, what does your team actually do today? Happy to keep it to 20 minutes.

### Follow-up 2 / close loop

Hi {{first_name}}, I’ll close the loop after this. If this is not a problem you own, is there someone in SEO/organic growth/product marketing/growth who would have a better firsthand view? No need to introduce us if not.

### Referral request after interview

Thanks again. You mentioned {{role/team}} also deals with this. If there is one person who has handled a recent example directly, an introduction would help me test whether what I heard from you repeats elsewhere.

### Qualified design-partner conversion

Based on the workflow you described, the next useful step may be a tightly scoped design-partner cycle rather than another discussion. The goal would be to configure five important buyer questions, run a live measurement, review one Recommendation Record and its evidence, assign one evidence-backed action, then repeat the exact-comparable measurement. We would define success/failure before starting. If that is worth committing time/data to, I can outline the scope for review.

Do not attach a price unless the current commercial hypothesis is intentionally being tested with that recipient and the scope is approved.

## Follow-up cadence

Default research cadence after approved sending scope:

- Day 0: personalized research invitation;
- Day 4–5: one relevance-based follow-up;
- Day 10–12: close-loop/referral note;
- stop unless the person replies or a materially new reason to contact them appears.

Quality overrides cadence. Do not automate high-volume cold outreach under this program.

## Research QA

Before counting an interview toward ICP/category evidence, check:

1. Participant/account are real and source-linked.
2. Qualification band is explicit.
3. Recent behavior was discussed.
4. Unaided category language was captured before explanation when category comprehension is being tested.
5. Notes separate behavior from opinion.
6. Evidence items preserve contradiction.
7. WTP/payment are not overstated.
8. Product analytics contains no raw PII/research text.
9. Confidence changes link actual evidence.
10. Sample size is reported with every synthesis.
