## Public-language clusters

Across these current alternative surfaces, the recurring language clusters are:

1. **Visibility / presence** — whether and how often the brand appears.
2. **AI Search / Answer Engines** — the environment where discovery happens.
3. **AEO / AI optimization** — the improvement discipline.
4. **Share of Voice / competitive presence** — relative position against competitors.
5. **Prompts / buyer questions** — what users ask and what the product measures.
6. **Citations / sources** — what evidence or source material appears behind answers.
7. **Actions / optimization** — what a marketing team should do next.

## What this means for Foremention experiments

**HYPOTHESIS, not customer evidence:** `Recommendation Intelligence` is more decision-oriented than the dominant public “AI Visibility/AEO” language. That may be differentiating, confusing, or both.

Do not rename the category from this desk research. Instead test whether qualified buyers:

- understand `Recommendation Intelligence` unaided;
- independently talk about “visibility,” “AI search,” “AEO,” “recommendations,” “shortlists,” “evidence,” “citations,” “share of voice,” or another frame;
- perceive Foremention’s emphasis on recommendation evidence + exact-comparable remeasurement + owned action as meaningfully different from a visibility dashboard;
- can explain that distinction without being coached;
- care enough about the distinction to commit time/data/workflow.

## Message-test implications

Keep the existing experiment order:

1. unaided `Recommendation Intelligence` comprehension;
2. problem → natural category language;
3. category → product comprehension;
4. category-first vs problem-first vs outcome-first vs competitive-intelligence vs recommendation-evidence framing.

Add explicit coding for the buyer volunteering any of these terms before Foremention introduces them:

- AI visibility;
- AI search;
- AEO / Answer Engine Optimization;
- GEO / Generative Engine Optimization;
- share of voice;
- recommendations;
- citations/sources/evidence;
- competitive intelligence;
- monitoring/tracking;
- optimization/action.

## Guardrails

- Competitor copy is **not** evidence of what buyers understand or prefer.
- Market prevalence of a term is **not** proof that Foremention should adopt it.
- Product overlap is **not** proof of substitution.
- Public pricing is **not** Foremention willingness-to-pay evidence.
- Customer logos/case studies on another company’s website are not Foremention proof.
- Any category change still requires qualified first-party buyer evidence.
