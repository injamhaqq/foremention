# Foremention Category Evidence

Working category: **Recommendation Intelligence**

Status: **🟡 DEFINED — NOT PROVEN**

Category hypothesis version: `CATEGORY-2026-09-v1`

Recommendation Intelligence remains the canonical working category until first-party evidence supports keeping, refining, or replacing it. Internal preference is not a reason to rename the category.

## What must be learned

We need first-party evidence for whether qualified buyers:

- understand the category unaided;
- recognize the underlying problem;
- care enough to act;
- distinguish Foremention from alternatives;
- naturally use similar or different language;
- understand Recommendation Intelligence without extensive explanation;
- perceive enough value to commit time/data/workflow, trial, or pay;
- return to the workflow after first value.

## Baseline confidence

**Category comprehension: NO EVIDENCE.** No verified qualified first-party category-comprehension observations have been recorded in the production evidence ledger yet.

Do not infer comprehension from website traffic, internal copy, investor reactions, search volume, or a product build.

## Experiment 1 — unaided comprehension

Audience: strongly ICP-relevant buyers before Foremention/category explanation.

Ask exactly:

> What would you expect a product called Recommendation Intelligence to do?

Capture the full unaided response first. Then code whether the person independently mentions any of:

- AI-mediated buyer recommendations;
- evidence/source support;
- competitive position or shortlist;
- repeated measurement/change;
- decisions/actions.

Do not correct them until the unaided answer has been recorded.

## Experiment 2 — problem → category

Describe the buyer problem without saying “Recommendation Intelligence” or Foremention.

Neutral problem description:

“Teams increasingly have important buyers asking AI systems discovery, comparison and shortlist questions. The team may need to know what gets recommended, what evidence appears behind those recommendations, what changed between comparable measurements, and what action should be owned next.”

Ask:

1. “How would you describe this problem in your own words?”
2. “What kind of product or service would you expect to solve it?”
3. “If you had to give that product category a name, what would you call it?”

Store natural language verbatim before showing any naming options.

## Experiment 3 — category → product comprehension

After unaided capture, show only **Recommendation Intelligence** and a short neutral explanation.

Measure separately, 1–5 only after the participant explains their reasoning:

- comprehension;
- relevance;
- urgency;
- differentiation;
- credibility;
- action intent.

A score is secondary evidence. The participant’s language and recent behavior are primary.

## Experiment 4 — message framing

Test distinct openings without declaring an early winner:

### Category headline

**Recommendation Intelligence for AI-mediated buying decisions.**

### Problem-first headline

**See what AI recommends for the buying questions that matter — and the evidence behind it.**

### Outcome-first headline

**Turn changing AI recommendations into evidence-backed actions your team can own and remeasure.**

### Competitive-intelligence framing

**Understand where competitors are being recommended in AI-driven discovery and comparison — then decide what to investigate next.**

### Recommendation-evidence framing

**Inspect the evidence behind AI recommendations, compare like-for-like measurements, and assign the next action.**

For every variant capture comprehension, relevance, urgency, differentiation, credibility and action intent. Do not treat click-through alone as category validation.

## Experiment 5 — demo opening

Compare:

- category-first opening;
- problem-first opening;
- buyer-question/evidence demonstration first.

Record what the participant asks next, where explanation is required, whether they connect the workflow to a job they own, and whether they commit to a next step.

## Experiment 6 — outreach wording

Compare category language with problem language only after a qualified target list and message scope are explicitly approved for sending.

Drafts may be prepared autonomously. **No outreach is sent from this program without founder approval of target list, message and sending scope.**

## Experiment 7 — buyer-language analysis

After a meaningful interview sample, code recurring buyer phrases without forcing them into Foremention vocabulary. Preserve:

- problem nouns;
- verbs used to describe the job;
- current-workaround language;
- trigger language;
- budget language;
- “why now” language;
- category names volunteered;
- competitor/alternative names volunteered;
- objections and confusion.

Only test alternative category names when repeated qualified interviews show material confusion or consistently stronger natural language.

## Experiment 8 — public alternative-language mapping

Public competitor/alternative messaging may inform test design, but is **VERIFIED PUBLIC FACT**, not FIRST-PARTY CUSTOMER EVIDENCE. Record what alternatives call themselves and what jobs they emphasize; never infer buyer preference from competitor copy.

## Interpretation guardrails

- Do not declare a message/category winner with an insufficient sample.
- Do not merge adjacent and strongly ICP-relevant participants to inflate comprehension.
- Do not count explanation-assisted understanding as unaided comprehension.
- Do not interpret polite interest as willingness to trial.
- Do not interpret a price opinion as verified payment.
- Do not interpret trial interest as activation.
- Do not interpret activation as repeat usage or retention.
- Preserve contradictory answers.

## Decision rules

At a checkpoint:

- **KEEP** Recommendation Intelligence when qualified buyers repeatedly understand it sufficiently and it helps them organize the problem without heavy explanation.
- **REFINE** when the underlying problem is understood but the category needs clarification, narrowing, or a descriptor.
- **PIVOT** when qualified buyers consistently use a materially different category frame that better maps to the recurring job and buying motion.
- **KILL** the category hypothesis when comprehension/relevance remain poor after adequate qualified testing and a different framing clearly performs better on behavior, not just wording preference.

## Current experiments

All category experiments are currently **PLANNED**, not completed. No winner exists. The first operational sequence is: unaided comprehension → problem-to-category → category-to-product → message framing. Alternative naming is intentionally deferred until evidence shows a reason to test it.
